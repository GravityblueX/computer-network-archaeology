# Authorship and AI Disclosure

## Primary author

The initial research plan, repository structure, catalog design, and large portions of the prose in this repository were written by **GPT-5.6 Sol, an OpenAI model**, beginning in August 2026.

This is intentional and should remain visible.

The project is an experiment in using a contemporary AI system as a research organizer for the archaeology of earlier information infrastructure. There is no attempt to disguise AI-written material as unaided human scholarship.

## Repository stewardship

The repository is maintained under the GitHub account **tmzncty**. Human contributors, reviewers, archivists, engineers and historians may add corrections, primary sources, memories, photographs, hardware details and bibliographic evidence.

## What the byline means

“Written by GPT-5.6 Sol” means the model may have:

- proposed the structure of the archive;
- synthesized information from cited sources;
- drafted explanatory prose;
- assembled inventories and research leads;
- identified contradictions that need investigation;
- generated cross-references and catalog metadata.

It does **not** mean that the model is itself a historical source.

## Evidence outranks authorship

Every substantive historical claim should eventually be supported by evidence. Preferred evidence includes contemporary technical reports, standards, RFCs, manuals, schematics, source code, operator documentation, meeting notes, oral histories, preserved hardware, and institutional records.

AI-generated prose without a traceable source should be treated as a research lead, not as final authority.

## Corrections

Corrections are welcome. Historical networking material is unusually vulnerable to:

- retrospective simplification;
- “first” claims with incompatible definitions;
- repeated folklore;
- vendor marketing claims;
- incorrect dates propagated between websites;
- renamed organizations and devices;
- undocumented hardware revisions;
- protocol names being used for both specifications and implementations;
- present-day terminology being projected backward.

When correcting a claim, preserve useful evidence about why the earlier wording was wrong whenever practical.

## Citation preference

Do not cite “GPT-5.6 Sol says…” as evidence for networking history. Cite the primary or secondary sources used by the model.

If this repository itself is cited as a synthesis, a suggested attribution is:

> GPT-5.6 Sol (OpenAI), *Computer Network Archaeology*, repository maintained by tmzncty, 2026–.
